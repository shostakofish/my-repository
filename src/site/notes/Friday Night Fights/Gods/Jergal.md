**Jergal**  
_The Scribe of the Doomed, Lord of the End of Everything, the Pitiless One_

- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral
- **Symbol:** A skull over a scroll
- **Domain:** Death, Knowledge
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Worshipers:** Keepers of records, undertakers, scribes, necromancers, seekers of undeath or divine order 

### Overview

Jergal is the ancient god of death, fate, and the final records of mortal lives. Once a greater deity, Jergal relinquished much of his power to Bhaal, Myrkul, and Bane in a game of knucklebones, preferring silent order to divine conflict. He remains the cold, methodical chronicler of the dead, maintaining the endless ledger of all who pass into the Fugue Plane.  
To his faithful, Jergal represents the inevitability of death and the importance of order in the face of chaos. He does not judge, only records. His temples are quiet, stone-walled sanctuaries where death is treated with solemnity and paperwork.  
Unlike most deities, Jergal neither encourages nor discourages followers; he simply endures. Those who seek him out are often drawn to his impassive nature and the knowledge that no one escapes his ink-stained hands.
 
### Tenets of Jergal

- **Record Without Passion:** Chronicle every end with impartial clarity. There is no place for emotion in the face of finality.
- **Respect Death's Order:** Undeath is not inherently evil, but it must serve purpose and balance. To unmake death is to unmake law.
- **Submit to the Ledger:** All things end. In the face of death, truth alone remains. 
### Myths and Lore

- **The Passing of the Mantle:** Jergal once held sway over all death, power, and strife, until he wearied of the vanity of divine rivalries. In a contest of fate, he gave his power to Bane, Bhaal, and Myrkul—preserving only the duties of scribe.
- **The Ink of Endings:** It is said Jergal writes every mortal’s name with a quill made from the first raven's feather and ink made of the last breath of the first being to die.
- **The Eternal Ledger:** Somewhere deep beneath the Fugue Plane lies the infinite vault, where Jergal inscribes the fate of all souls. Only the dead may read its pages—and only the gods may question them. 
### Clergy and Temples

Temples to Jergal are dim, orderly sanctuaries filled with scrolls, ossuaries, and quiet halls. Each shrine houses a chronicle room where deaths are recorded by hand, and a sanctum for funeral rites.  
Clerics of Jergal wear gray robes and bone-carved holy symbols, and often serve as gravekeepers, morticians, or keepers of city records. Their devotion lies in structure, finality, and the accuracy of the written word.
 
### Relationships

Jergal serves Kelemvor without complaint, acting as seneschal and record-keeper of the dead. He has little regard for the passions of Bhaal, Myrkul, or Cyric, viewing them as symptoms of mortal arrogance. Among deities of knowledge and law such as Oghma, Deneir, and Helm, he is respected but rarely welcomed.  
Even gods of undeath like Velsharoon and Orcus approach Jergal with caution, knowing that his ledgers neither lie nor forget.
 
### Signs of Worship

- Quiet stone shrines with scrolls organized by date of death
- An abundance of skull imagery, always clean and polished
- Funeral rites performed with emphasis on documentation
- Gravediggers or priests murmuring names of the dead while writing

|---|---|

|Record the death of a creature with accurate detail.|Destroy or falsify death records.|

|Aid a city or temple in organizing its records or tombs.|Disturb graves for profit or personal gain.|
|Bring righteous end to those who desecrate the lives and deaths of others.|Kill wantonly without respect, reason, or thought.|

|Serve as a neutral witness in a death-related dispute.|Spread chaos in the handling of the dead.|
|Explore the knowledge of ancient texts or rites.|Refuse to record or speak a death that has occurred.|
|Uphold Jergal's laws in funerary practice or clerical order.|Treat death as a joke or spectacle.|
