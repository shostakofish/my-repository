_The Oak Father, the Forest Father, Treefather, Old Oak_

- **Alignment:** Neutral
- **Symbol:** A green living oak leaf
- **Domain:** Nature
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Worshipers:** Druids, rangers, farmers, foresters, elementalists, protectors of wilderness
- **Favored Weapon:** Greatclub (often carved from sacred trees)   

### Overview

Silvanus is the ancient and primeval god of wild nature and its impartial balance. He is the embodiment of the untamed wilderness, the seasonal cycle of life and death, and the deep, primal power of the earth itself. Neither cruel nor kind, Silvanus defends natural order above all, permitting no unnecessary destruction, but accepting that death feeds life.  
His worshipers strive to protect natural places from encroachment, preserve the balance between predators and prey, and understand the sacredness of growth, decay, and rebirth. Silvanus's presence can be felt in ancient groves, roaring waterfalls, storm-swept plains, and even lightning-touched trees.  
He is often worshiped alongside Chauntea, Mielikki, and Eldath, though he takes on a more remote and cosmic role among nature gods.
   

### Tenets of Silvanus

- **Preserve the Balance:** All life is part of the cycle. Do not disrupt it needlessly.
- **Protect the Wilds:** Let no city, road, or greed swallow the last untouched places.
- **Accept Death and Change:** The forest burns, the tree falls, the new seed grows. This is the way.
- **Revere the Primeval:** Mountains, storms, rivers, beasts—all these are sacred forms of Silvanus.   

### Myths and Lore

- **The First Grove:** Silvanus is said to have shaped the world's first forest before any other god walked the earth, his breath sprouting oaks and pines from bare stone.
- **The Storm That Spared No Tree:** In an ancient myth, Silvanus sent a hurricane across Faerûn, uprooting even sacred groves, to remind mortals that nature's wrath spares none.
- **The Pact of Tooth and Leaf:** Silvanus forged an eternal pact between beasts and forests, ensuring that predators do not destroy their hunting grounds and prey always have refuge.   

### Clergy and Temples

Temples to Silvanus are rarely buildings; most are outdoor circles of ancient standing stones, natural clearings, or moss-covered shrines deep within the woods. His sacred sites often include a massive central tree or stone and are tended by druids and wild-priests.  
Clerics of Silvanus wear robes in shades of brown and green, often adorned with vines, feathers, and bones. They wield wooden staves or greatclubs and are usually trained in survival, nature lore, and herbalism.  
These priests are expected to maintain balance, protect sacred places, and intervene only when the wilderness is unjustly harmed.
   

### Relationships

Silvanus is closely aligned with nature deities like Mielikki (his daughter), Eldath, and Chauntea. While he respects Chauntea's cultivation of the land, he favors wild nature over farms. He disdains Talos for his destructive chaos and opposes Malar, who promotes senseless slaughter.  
Though distant, he respects the elemental forces and often works with earth and water spirits. He is indifferent to most non-nature gods unless their actions directly harm the wild.
   

### Signs of Worship

- Ancient trees marked with sacred runes or vines braided around trunks
- Animal tracks circling a stone or fallen tree in unnatural patterns
- Sudden calm in the wild as if the forest itself is watching
- Grove guardians or awakened plants near old ruins   

### Earning and Losing Favor

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Actions That Gain Favor**|**Actions That Lose Favor**|
|Planting and tending native flora in a damaged ecosystem|Burning or razing forests for profit|
|Defending a grove, glade, or wild animal from harm|Polluting rivers or sacred springs|
|Reintroducing balance between predator and prey|Littering and poisoning nature|
|Teaching others to respect the wilderness|Spreading civilization unchecked into wilderness areas|
|Preserving old growth trees or sacred sites|Desecrating druidic or natural sanctuaries|
|Leading someone to safety through the wilderness|Killing for sport or trophy|
|Speaking or singing prayers in druidic at dawn or dusk|Enslaving beasts or elemental creatures|
|Offering natural items (acorns, river stones, feathers) to shrines|Disrupting peaceful natural places|
